# Scam and phishing risk evaluation

Generated by `python -m scripts.evaluate_risk --report` from the CC0 synthetic benchmark in `fixtures/phishing_benchmark/` (built by `scripts/build_phishing_benchmark.py`). The run is local and deterministic: re-running it reproduces this file byte for byte.

- Cases: 178 (120 train, 58 test), from 33 templates in 24 families. Each template appears as an HTML email and as a plain-text email.
- The split is **by template**: no test email shares wording with a training email.
- Score: logistic regression over the findings, weights >= 0, L2 0.05. Bands: high >= 70, medium >= 40.

## Limits

- The benchmark and the rules were written by the same person, so these numbers are optimistic. They show the checks work as designed on unseen templates, not that they generalise to every scam.
- 58 test cases give wide uncertainty: one extra error moves a rate by 2 to 4 points.
- Lookalike matching trades recall for false alarms on innocent names one letter from a brand (`tomato.in` resembles `zomato.com`) or containing a short brand word (`axis-design.com`). Domains you label Important or Updates, or that sent you at least two such emails and no spam, are trusted and never count as lookalikes; a repeated scam campaign filed as Updates therefore stops being flagged as a lookalike from its third email, while every other check still applies.
- Small businesses without DMARC that ask for payment by a deadline and reply from Gmail look like scams to every rule here (the `small_business` false positives above). The report shows the reasons so you can judge; MailMind never hides or deletes mail because of this score.
- The more meaningful number is the false-positive rate on your own mail. Run `python -m scripts.evaluate_risk --inbox`: it prints band counts for your saved mail and never writes them anywhere.
